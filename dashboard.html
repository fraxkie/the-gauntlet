02

Direction is a coin flip — but volatility is predictable

The single most important finding. Predicting which way the market goes tomorrow is nearly impossible. But predicting how volatile tomorrow will be is genuinely tractable — because volatility clusters. The forecast (blue) tracks realized volatility (grey) with 0.65 correlation, fully out-of-sample, beating the naive baseline by 27%. This is the first real predictive signal in the project.

03

That forecast makes risk controllable

Because volatility is predictable, position size can be scaled inversely to it — smaller when storms are coming, larger when calm. The result isn't higher returns; it's dramatically steadier risk. Volatility-targeting (orange) cut the maximum drawdown from −55% to −35% versus simply holding (grey), with a higher Sharpe ratio. Surviving drawdowns is how you stay in the game.

The engine. Five validation firewalls (Monte Carlo permutation, walk-forward consistency, regime segmentation, look-ahead audit, multiple-comparison ledger) plus a probability pipeline (volatility forecasting, Hidden Markov regime detection, calibration). Every component proven on known-answer synthetic data before being trusted on real markets. Built from scratch in Python — the HMM, the permutation tests, and the calibration math are all hand-implemented.

The finding. Simple price-based strategies on liquid markets are overwhelmingly disguised beta. Real, out-of-sample signal lives in volatility and regimes — not in predicting direction. Methods after Timothy Masters (permutation tests), López de Prado (multiple testing), and standard regime/volatility econometrics.
